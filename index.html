Health analyst & writer  ·  Bilingual EN/ES  ·  Wellington & remote

Health analysis and writing, with a decade inside the clinic.

I am a bilingual health analyst and writer, in English and Spanish. I spent ten years working in dental practices in Wellington, and then went back to study. In 2025 I finished a Postgraduate Diploma in Public Health at the University of Otago with distinction. My training covers policy, biostatistics, health promotion, and regulatory writing, and I have written analyses on the health systems of the US, Singapore, and Aotearoa. I work with organisations that need their health information to be both accurate and easy to read.

Open to permanent and fixed-term roles Available for contract and freelance work Remote globally

What I can work on

  • 01
    Oral health policy and the dental sector The dental sector in New Zealand and the policy that shapes it. I look at workforce shortages, the cost of care, fluoridation debates, and how a clinic runs day to day. Useful for dental practices, dental groups, regulators, and policy teams who want analysis from someone who has worked inside a clinic.
  • 02
    Health equity, Indigenous health, and Te Tiriti o Waitangi Practical work for organisations whose patients, members, or communities are reached least well by the health system. In Aotearoa I work with Te Tiriti and Māori health frameworks like Te Whare Tapa Whā and Te Pae Mahutonga. For international work I use Indigenous health frameworks more broadly, and for the US and global market I work in the language of health equity and social determinants of health.
  • 03
    Regulatory writing and clinical compliance I can write health impact assessments, regulatory impact assessments, and parliamentary submissions. I can also write the kind of documentation that small healthcare practices need: risk registers, infection prevention and control documents, audit preparation, and incident reporting.
  • 04
    Quantitative methods I can help agencies understand their data with complex statistical techniques such as regression models with diagnostics, and turning statistical findings into language non-technical readers can act on.
  • 05
    Global and comparative health systems Comparison of high-income health systems, with particular attention to the US, Australia, New Zealand, and Singapore. Useful for organisations planning international expansion, entering a new market, or thinking about system-level reform.

Comparative Health Systems Analysis

Healthcare in the United States and Singapore

A structured comparison of two very different high-income health systems. The US spends about three times more per person than Singapore, but performs worse on the major health indicators. The piece works through why, and what the comparison says about how health systems should be designed.
