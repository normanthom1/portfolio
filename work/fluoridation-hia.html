Health Impact Assessment

Community Water Fluoridation in Wellington & Taranaki

A full Health Impact Assessment, with an Environmental Health Risk Assessment appendix, prepared in support of an iwi-led submission on the proposed Fluoridation Referendum Bill.

Academic context. Prepared as the major assessment for the Health Protection paper of the Postgraduate Diploma in Public Health at the University of Otago (2025). Written in role as an analyst supporting Te Āti Awa's submission to a parliamentary committee. The diploma was completed with distinction overall.

About this project

In early 2024 a private member's bill (the Fluoridation Referendum Bill) was proposed to reverse the 2021 Health (Fluoridation of Drinking Water) Amendment Act, which had moved decision-making authority on community water fluoridation from local councils to the Director-General of Health. Reverting to local referenda would, in practice, mean some councils would discontinue fluoridation in their water supplies.

For Te Āti Awa, whose rohe spans both Wellington and Taranaki, the proposed Bill carried specific implications for the populations they advocate for. This HIA was prepared in the role of an analyst supporting Te Āti Awa's submission, to evaluate the population health consequences of the proposed change and to ground the submission in a structured assessment of risks, benefits, and equity impacts.

Approach

The HIA followed the structure set out in the Ministry of Health's Guide to Health Impact Assessment: screening (justifying the use of the HIA approach), scoping (defining the health lens and stakeholders), and identification and assessment of impacts. The geographical scope was narrowed to Wellington and Taranaki, in line with Te Āti Awa's area of interest.

The assessment was paired with an Environmental Health Risk Assessment appendix that ran systematically through hazard identification (for both the presence and absence of fluoride), dose-response assessment (drawing on the WHO drinking water quality guidelines and the 2014 Royal Society of New Zealand review), exposure assessment in the New Zealand context, risk characterisation, and risk management. The dual structure was deliberate: HIA captures the population health and equity story; EHRA grounds it in the underlying toxicological evidence.

Key findings

  • Removing fluoride from community water supplies would predictably increase dental caries prevalence, with disproportionate effects on populations who already face significant barriers to accessing dental care, particularly Māori and Pasifika children.
  • Year 8 oral health data shows higher caries-free rates in fluoridated regions, and the gap is larger for Māori and Pasifika children than for non-Māori non-Pasifika children. Removing fluoride therefore widens rather than narrows existing oral health inequities.
  • The intervention is unusually low-friction: it operates at the water-supply level rather than at the level of individual behaviour, which is why its protective effect is strongest for populations with the least access to dental services.
  • Removing universal protective coverage without simultaneously providing free or universal access to dental services would represent a breach of the Crown's obligations under Te Tiriti o Waitangi, particularly the principles of equity (Article 3) and active protection (Article 2).
  • The assessment recommended retaining centralised decision-making under the Director-General of Health, rejecting the Fluoridation Referendum Bill, and applying a Te Tiriti o Waitangi lens to all subsequent oral health policy decisions.

What this project demonstrates

Beyond the substantive findings, this piece demonstrates capability with a specific public health methodology (HIA) and a specific environmental health methodology (EHRA) used in combination, an applied use of Te Tiriti o Waitangi as an analytical framework rather than a citation, and the ability to translate technical risk assessment into the language of a parliamentary submission.
