## 1. The honest assessment

Your portfolio is genuinely well-built. The writing is mature, the work samples are substantive, the design is restrained and professional, and the Te Tiriti framing reads as authentic rather than performative. That is more than most candidate sites achieve.

It is also commercially narrow in a way that is going to cost you money.

You have positioned yourself as **a junior public health analyst seeking permanent or fixed-term roles in the NZ public sector, universities, or policy consultancies.** That is a precise positioning for a specific market — and that market is currently both contracting (public sector cuts running through 2024–2026) and credentialed against you at the next tier (most research roles want a PhD). You have correctly identified that this is the problem.

The fix is not to "sound more commercial." The fix is to recognise that you have **two distinct profiles bundled into one page**, and the second one is the more commercially valuable of the two.

**Profile A (currently the headline):** Postgraduate Diploma in Public Health, distinction, with Te Tiriti-grounded analytical training. Hireable by: MoH, Te Whatu Ora, Health NZ regions, iwi providers, some NGOs, some policy consultancies, some university research teams. Market is shrinking.

**Profile B (currently the subordinate clause):** A bilingual (EN/ES native) practitioner with ten years inside the NZ oral health sector, a cosmetic dentistry diploma, and postgraduate training in regulatory writing, biostatistics, and policy analysis. Hireable by: dental practices, dental groups, dental marketing agencies, health/wellness brands, telehealth, supplement businesses, US health systems with Latino patient bases, allied health practices, health insurers, regulatory compliance consultancies, and herself as a freelance operator. Market is large, growing, and hungry for credible health writers.

Your site sells Profile A and buries Profile B in the "ten years in Wellington's oral health system" paragraph. Flip that.

## 2. What is limiting your employability right now

Going through the site directly.

**The homepage tagline is jargon outside govt and academia.** "Public health policy, with a clinical lens" reads beautifully to a public health professional and reads as nothing to anyone else. A dental practice owner does not know what a public health analyst does. A wellness brand founder does not either. They both, however, know what a "health writer" or a "regulatory specialist" is.

**Every work sample is coursework.** You flag this honestly, which protects your integrity, but for a commercial buyer it reads: "no real projects yet." Until you have at least 2–3 paid or pro-bono engagements with named (or anonymised) clients on the site, commercial buyers will discount you against people who have less talent but more proof.

**No commercial outcomes language anywhere.** Across five pages and four project write-ups, there is not one sentence about dollars saved, hours returned, conversion lifted, complaints reduced, claims defended, or revenue protected. Everything is framed as analytical rigour. That is correct for academic readers and weak for commercial ones.

**The dental decade is treated as backstory.** "I treat that decade as a genuine credential, not a stepping-stone" is the right *thought*, but the site is still organised around the analyst hat. Commercially, the ten years is the more rare and valuable asset. Almost no one with a public health PgDip also has operator-level fluency in how dental clinics run. That combination is the moat.

**Te Tiriti positioning, while honest, narrows your addressable market.** This needs to be said carefully because the values are real and you should not abandon them. But: leading with "Te Tiriti-grounded health analysis" on the homepage signals to commercial readers (a) "I do Crown work" and (b) "I will scrutinise your work through this lens whether you asked or not." Both are correct for the Crown buyer. Both filter out wellness brands, dental practices, and small businesses. The fix is not to remove Te Tiriti work — it is to stop putting it in the homepage tagline. Let it sit in your About page and your case studies, where buyers who want that frame will find it and buyers who don't will not be repelled at the door.

**Spanish is buried.** Native EN/ES with a decade in NZ healthcare is rare. It is currently mentioned at the bottom of pages, in passing. That should be a top-of-page asset — a lever into US Latino health markets, ANZ Latino patient populations, and LatAm wellness brands entering ANZ.

**Geographic positioning is excessively local.** "Wellington, Aotearoa New Zealand" is on every page. Your work is almost entirely remote-deliverable. The site should signal you can serve AU, US, and LatAm clients.

**The contact page lists what you are "open to," not what you sell.** "Coffee with researchers" is networking language. Commercial buyers do not book coffee; they book services. Until the site has a services page with concrete offers and prices (or at least price ranges), no commercial buyer will reach out. They will read the contact page, see "permanent roles, fixed-term roles, and project work like literature reviews and HIAs," and conclude you are not for them.

**Work samples are organised by methodology, not by problem solved.** "Health Impact Assessment," "Parliamentary Submission," "Quantitative Analysis" — these are deliverable types. Commercial buyers organise their thinking by problem ("I need to defend my product claims," "I need to communicate with patients better," "I need to figure out why my hygiene revenue is flat"). Reframe at least one or two samples by the problem they would solve for a commercial buyer.

**Why a practice pays.** Drives bookings. Reduces no-shows. Reduces post-op call volume. Demonstrates competence to patients. Protects against complaints from miscommunication. Frees owner-clinician time.

**Ideal customer.** 1–6 chair AU/NZ independent practices, recently bought practices (the owner is overwhelmed), corporate dental groups for white-label work (Lumino, Abano, Smile Dental, 1300 Smiles), and dental marketing agencies needing a clinical writer.

**Package.** Monthly retainer: 4 blog/newsletter articles + 8 social posts + 2 patient education one-pagers + 1 recall letter rewrite per month. One-off: "Patient communication library" — a full set of post-op, pre-op, financing, and recall templates the practice keeps forever.

**Pricing (starting out).** Intro client: NZD 800/mo to build a case study. Standard: NZD 1,500–3,500/mo retainer. One-off library: NZD 4,000–8,000.

**Expert positioning without a big brand.** Do not claim influence. Claim experience: "Ten years assisting in Wellington dental practices plus postgraduate public health training" beats "agency-trained copywriter" with a practice owner every time.

**Credibility anchors.** Decade as DA across multiple practices; Diploma in Cosmetic Dentistry (Universidad de Buenos Aires); your writing samples; the "Ten years as a dental assistant" essay as proof you can write about clinics.

**Recurring revenue potential.** Strong. Retainer model. Three retainers at NZD 2,500/mo = NZD 90,000/yr.

## 10. AI and automation alignment

This is where your edge is real and durable, not where you compete with the AI.

You should not be selling "I write content" against ChatGPT. You will lose on price. You should be selling **"I am the human gate between AI output and a regulator, a patient, or a complainant."** AI plus an expert reviewer is the production model for the next five years in health, and the expert reviewer is the part that is scarce.

Concrete AI-leveraged offerings:

**Bilingual content at scale.** LLM-assisted first-draft translation + your transcreation review. You produce three times the volume at the same quality bar. Your hourly economics improve dramatically without lowering your rate.

**Claims compliance audits.** LLM to scan all marketing copy across a brand and flag potential issues; you review the flagged items and produce a written audit. Productisable into a fixed-scope "$2,500 wellness brand claims audit" that takes you a day.

**Patient communication template engines.** Build a library of clinically accurate templates per procedure, plus a brief intake-form-based personalisation layer. Sell to practices as a one-off ($X) plus a small monthly licence.

**Practice metric digest.** Pull data from practice management software, generate a monthly written digest with insights and recommendations. AI does the writing; you do the interpretation and pattern recognition. Recurring revenue at low time cost.

**Submission assembly.** LLM for first-draft assembly of routine submissions from a structured intake; you do the substantive argument and the regulatory framing. Cuts the labour cost of a submission by maybe 60%.

The thread: **AI handles the boilerplate; you handle the parts a regulator, a patient, or a court would actually evaluate.** That is the position that holds up.
