## 1. The positioning I see your site targeting now

Your site is positioned as a bilingual (English/Spanish) health analyst and writer who combines ten years of clinical operations experience with formal postgraduate training in public health, biostatistics, regulatory writing, and global health systems. The audience the site appears to be reaching for, based on the work samples, the services page, and the recent restructuring, is:

- Health policy consultancies in NZ and internationally
- Telehealth and health-tech companies, especially those expanding to Latino markets
- US health systems and patient-education publishers serving Latino populations
- Tobacco control and global health NGOs
- Dental SaaS, dental groups, and dental industry communications
- Health journalism and explainer publishers

Your Blog page currently shows two pieces (the Colombia football post and the indigenous Medellín housing post). Both are good writing, both demonstrate genuine subject matter, but neither is doing direct discoverability work for the buyer markets above. To strengthen authority across those markets, the blog needs to cover ground in the buyer's vocabulary, not only in your own.

The five posts below are designed to do that.

## 2. Five strategic blog post ideas

### Post 1: "Spanish-language health content is mostly translated, not transcreated. Patients can tell."

**Target audience.** US health system content leads, US Latino health publishers (Salud America!, Healthline Spanish edition, Mayo Clinic en Español, Cleveland Clinic Spanish), telehealth companies expanding into Latino markets (Hims & Hers, BetterHelp, Talkspace), and health communications agencies (Real Chemistry, Klick Health).

**Core topic.** The gap between literal translation of patient education materials from English to Spanish, and proper transcreation that reads as native and respects the clinical specifics. Real examples of common patterns where translation introduces clinical ambiguity (e.g., medication instructions, post-op care, mental health screening prompts) and proposes a checklist for transcreation review.

**Why it supports your career direction.** This is the post that most directly demonstrates the value of your bilingual + clinical + public health stack. The international outreach strategy lists US Latino health content as the single largest underserved buyer market for your profile. A piece on this topic is your strongest cold-email asset, because it shows rather than tells the case for hiring you over a generic translator.

**How it improves credibility, visibility, SEO.** Keywords like "Spanish health content", "Latino patient education", "health translation vs transcreation", "Spanish patient communication" have meaningful long-tail search volume in the US and are not crowded with credentialed voices. A piece with a checklist or framework also tends to get linked to by health communicators, which compounds.

**Search intent and keywords.** Spanish-language patient education, transcreation vs translation, Latino health literacy, health content for Spanish speakers, bilingual patient communication, Latino health equity.

**Outline.**

1. The problem in one example: a single mistranslated mental health screening prompt and what it does to a clinician's read of the patient
2. Why direct translation is the default and where it breaks: idioms, register, culturally loaded health concepts, regional Spanish variation
3. What transcreation looks like in practice: working from the underlying clinical intent rather than the English text
4. A short checklist for organisations commissioning Spanish patient education: who writes it, who reviews it, what kind of clinical sign-off is needed
5. Close with a paragraph on what the bilingual + clinical combination contributes, and an invitation to reach out

**Recommended call to action.** A two-sentence offer at the end inviting US health systems or publishers who commission Spanish content to get in touch, with a link to your contact page and a mention that you take on commissioned writing and transcreation work.

### Post 2: "Ten years inside dental practices: what public health analysis usually misses about how dental care gets delivered"

**Target audience.** Dental SaaS companies (Weave, Solutionreach, Curve Dental, Tebra, Dentrix), dental practice operators (Lumino, Abano, AU and US DSOs), dental industry analysts and communicators, and oral health policy researchers.

**Core topic.** The reality of how a dental practice operates day to day, and the specific things that public health analysis of the dental sector typically gets wrong because the analysts have never been at the chair. Examples: how recall systems actually work, why no-shows happen, how treatment plan acceptance is influenced by the staff member doing the explaining, what infection prevention looks like in practice versus on paper, why workforce shortages have downstream effects that do not show up in workforce data.

**Why it supports your career direction.** Your dental clinical decade is your most distinctive credential. Almost no other PgDip graduate has it. A piece that demonstrates the analytical edge that experience produces is the strongest commercial argument for hiring you over a more academically traditional analyst. It also opens the dental SaaS market, which the outreach strategy identifies as underexploited.

**How it improves credibility, visibility, SEO.** "Dental practice operations", "dental sector analysis", "oral health policy NZ", "dental workforce shortage" are all keyword clusters with decent search volume and not much competition from people with both clinical and analytical training. Dental SaaS marketing teams actively look for content like this for their own publications and for credible authors to feature.

**Search intent and keywords.** Dental practice management, oral health policy, dental workforce, dental sector operations, patient communication dental, dental access cost.

**Outline.**

1. Open with a specific concrete example: a patient who was lost to follow-up and what the data shows about that pattern versus what the chair shows
2. Three things public health analysis usually misses: workforce attrition signals invisible in headcount data; recall and treatment acceptance dynamics; the operational reality of infection prevention and clinical compliance
3. What the analytical lens gains when you have actually been at the chair
4. Where this matters for dental sector policy, for dental SaaS product design, and for workforce planning
5. Close with what the combination of clinical decade plus formal public health training can produce for a buyer

**Recommended call to action.** A two-sentence note offering analysis or content writing for dental SaaS companies, dental groups, or dental policy teams that want analysis from someone who has worked in clinics. Link to your contact page.

### Post 3: "What the US health system looks like from the outside, and why Singapore is the comparison that actually matters"

**Target audience.** US health-tech founders considering market entry, international health consultancies (DAI, Health Partners International, Oxford Policy Management), global health journalism readers (KFF Health News, STAT, The Conversation), and policy teams at US safety-net systems or FQHCs.

**Core topic.** A short, accessible explainer pulling from your PUBH 742 paper. Why the standard "US health spending is high but outcomes are low" framing is incomplete, what Singapore's 3M system actually does, and what the comparison says about the political conditions under which evidence-based health policy actually gets implemented. The piece should not try to declare a winner. It should give a non-US reader a clear mental model of how the US system is structured, and a non-Singapore reader the same for the 3M system.

**Why it supports your career direction.** This piece is the strongest commissionable explainer on your site. It establishes you as someone who can write the kind of cross-border health systems comparison that KFF Health News, The Conversation, and STAT regularly commission. It is also the single best cold-email asset for the international consulting bucket in the outreach strategy doc, because it shows you can take complex systems content and make it readable for a non-specialist.

**How it improves credibility, visibility, SEO.** "US health system explained", "Singapore 3M health system", "comparative health systems", "US healthcare for non-Americans" are all keyword clusters with steady long-tail traffic and surprisingly thin credentialed coverage outside of paywalled academic journals.

**Search intent and keywords.** Comparative health systems analysis, US healthcare explained, Singapore Medisave, MediShield Life, US vs Singapore health spending, healthcare system design.

**Outline.**

1. Open with the headline contrast: the US spends about three times more per person than Singapore and performs worse on the major indicators
2. The structure of the US system in five sentences: multi-payer, private insurance with public programmes for over-65 (Medicare) and certain low-income (Medicaid), the uninsured gap, the political economy that makes reform hard
3. The structure of the Singapore 3M system in five sentences: Medisave individual savings, MediShield Life catastrophic insurance, Medifund safety net, and the political conditions that allowed it
4. What the comparison actually shows: not that one is "better" but that each system produces predictable outcomes given its inputs, and the inputs are political and structural rather than technical
5. The implication for any health-tech company or policy team thinking about a new market: which features of the system you cannot change determine what your intervention can actually do

**Recommended call to action.** A short invitation to organisations doing international expansion or comparative health systems work to get in touch, plus a link to the full paper on your Work page.

### Post 4: "The 2024 New Zealand smokefree repeal, one year on: what the modelling predicted, what actually happened, and what restoration would need to look like"

**Target audience.** International tobacco control NGOs (Vital Strategies, Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids, Truth Initiative, Action on Smoking and Health), public health journalists (The Conversation, STAT, BMJ Opinion), NZ public health audience (Hāpai te Hauora, Heart Foundation NZ, ASH NZ), and global tobacco control researchers.

**Core topic.** A short, evidence-led look at what the first year of post-repeal data is showing in New Zealand. The 2024 repeal removed retail outlet reduction, denicotinisation, and the smokefree generation. The international tobacco control community has been watching closely. This piece would summarise the early evidence, contrast it with what the original modelling predicted, and lay out the political and operational conditions a credible restoration would need.

**Why it supports your career direction.** Tobacco control is one of the strongest international markets for your stack. The outreach strategy doc identifies Vital Strategies, Allen + Clarke, and the WHO FCTC Secretariat as direct targets. A timely, credible blog post on the New Zealand repeal one year on is the single best piece of bait for those conversations. It is also a strong commissioned-piece pitch for The Conversation NZ or Vital Strategies' own blog.

**How it improves credibility, visibility, SEO.** Keywords like "New Zealand smokefree repeal", "denicotinisation", "smokefree generation", "tobacco endgame" are tightly clustered around your work and are searched by tobacco control researchers and journalists globally. The repeal is internationally newsworthy. A piece written by someone with a portfolio submission on the topic is a credible commission and a credible search result.

**Search intent and keywords.** New Zealand smokefree repeal, tobacco endgame New Zealand, denicotinisation modelling, smokefree generation policy, tobacco control NZ 2025.

**Outline.**

1. Open with the most concrete change in the data: daily smoker numbers, hospitalisations, or quit-line traffic, depending on what is publicly available
2. What the original modelling (Wilson, Hoek, van der Deen and colleagues) predicted for the framework, and what we are seeing instead
3. The political and operational conditions that would need to be in place for a credible restoration
4. The international read: what other tobacco control communities are learning from the New Zealand experience
5. Close with a paragraph on what evidence-based policy requires when an evidence-based policy is repealed

**Recommended call to action.** A short note inviting tobacco control organisations or journalists working on this issue to get in touch. Link to the full submission on the Work page.

### Post 5: "What clinical experience adds to public health analysis (and what gets missed when neither side talks to the other)"

**Target audience.** Health policy consultancies, public health think tanks, public health units, healthcare communications agencies, and any organisation thinking about analyst hiring. Also recruiters, hiring managers, and anyone who lands on your site after a personal search.

**Core topic.** A career-positioning essay that crystallises your unique value proposition. The thesis: public health policy in most countries is written by people with policy training but no clinical experience, while clinical care is delivered by people with clinical training but limited exposure to policy mechanics. The thin overlap is where the most useful analysis happens, and most organisations underinvest in it. The piece would use specific examples from your work (fluoridation HIA, smokefree submission, oral health affordability) to show what the overlap actually produces.

**Why it supports your career direction.** This is your brand-positioning piece. Every other blog post argues for hiring you on a specific topic. This one argues for hiring you in general. It is the piece a hiring manager or potential client lands on after Googling you, and it is the piece you can link to in every other outreach email.

**How it improves credibility, visibility, SEO.** It targets the most valuable search query of all for you: people searching your name plus "public health" or "analyst" or "consulting". It also captures longer-tail searches like "clinical experience in public health analysis" and "health policy from the chair side". And it is the natural piece to send as the about-me link in any application or pitch.

**Search intent and keywords.** Public health analyst, clinical public health, health policy clinical realism, public health career change clinician, health policy from clinical experience.

**Outline.**

1. Open with a specific example where a clinically informed reading changed the analytical conclusion of a policy question
2. Why most public health analysis is done without clinical input: not by design, but as a function of how training pipelines work
3. What the clinical lens adds: workforce signals invisible in workforce data, treatment acceptance dynamics, infection prevention as an operational rather than policy question
4. What the policy lens adds back to the clinic: structural attribution, regulatory framing, evidence synthesis
5. Close with a clear statement of what the combination produces, and the kinds of organisations and questions it fits

**Recommended call to action.** Direct: link to your Services page and Contact page, with a one-sentence offer to talk to anyone whose work sits in the clinical / policy overlap.

## 3. Gaps and missed positioning opportunities on the current site

These are things the five posts above can help close.

**You claim bilingual but your blog has no Spanish content.** Right now a US Latino health buyer landing on your site has no Spanish writing sample to evaluate. Post 1 (Spanish-language health content) is the natural fix; ideally publish it bilingually, with the Spanish version available either side-by-side or as a separate URL.

**Your dental decade is buried.** Item 01 on the homepage now treats clinical experience as the credential rather than the topic, which is correct. But the deeper authority piece, the one a dental SaaS marketing lead would link out to, does not exist yet. Post 2 (ten years inside dental practices) is that piece.

**Your US-market signal is one paper deep.** The US/Singapore work is your strongest international asset, but it sits in a sidebar on the Work page. Post 3 (US health system from the outside) turns it into a discoverable explainer that any US-facing buyer can read in five minutes.

**Your tobacco control authority is one paper deep too.** The smokefree submission is strong but it is academic and old. Post 4 (smokefree repeal one year on) turns it into current-affairs commentary, which is what NGOs and journalists actually link to.

**Your unique value proposition is not articulated anywhere as a single readable thing.** The site lists capabilities, lists work, lists offers. None of these tells the reader, in a paragraph, why your combination is rare and useful. Post 5 (clinical experience plus public health analysis) is that paragraph.

**No content cadence visible.** Two posts is enough to demonstrate writing, but not enough to look "active". Five posts, spread over three to four months, signals an active writer to anyone deciding whether you are still in the game. The Coming Soon block on the Blog page should be replaced or shortened once a third post lands.

**No lead magnet.** Right now a visitor who reads a post has one path: the contact form. A simple downloadable checklist or one-pager (e.g., "How to commission Spanish-language patient education content: a checklist") attached to post 1 would dramatically increase the conversion of readers to inbound enquiries. You can build this in an hour.

**Services page is conservative on pricing.** The intro-pricing framing is honest and right for now, but a reader who lands on Services from a blog post is more likely to convert if they see at least one piece of work explicitly priced. Adding "From NZD 1,500 for a transcreation audit" or similar on Service 2 would change the conversion rate.

**LinkedIn link is a placeholder.** The Contact page has `linkedin.com/in/johannaescudero` as a non-functional link. That is the easiest thing on this list to fix: replace with the actual profile URL and make it open in a new tab.

## 4. Which to write first

If you wrote them in this order, the leverage compounds.

**First:** post 5 (clinical experience + public health analysis). It is your brand-positioning piece and once it exists you can link to it from every other outreach email and every later post.

**Second:** post 3 (US health system from the outside). It is your strongest international asset turned into something searchable.

**Third:** post 1 (Spanish-language health content). It is your strongest commercial argument for the US Latino health market, the highest-paid international segment.

**Fourth:** post 4 (smokefree repeal one year on). Timely. Best pitched to The Conversation NZ or BMJ Opinion as a paid commission rather than only published on your own blog.

**Fifth:** post 2 (ten years inside dental practices). The longest of the five but the most defensible. Saves the dental SaaS market for when you have the other four pieces already published.

You do not need to write all five at once. Two of them this month, one a month thereafter, and you will have a publication rhythm that signals active practice without burning you out.
