## 1. The honest read on where you fit, internationally

The market for "junior public health analyst" is small everywhere. The market for the stack you actually have, when described properly, is much larger. The stack is:

- Native Spanish + near-native English, with clinical fluency in both
- Ten years operational experience inside a regulated clinical setting (dental)
- Postgraduate Diploma in Public Health, with distinction, covering policy, biostatistics, health promotion, regulatory writing, health protection, and global health systems
- Distinction-level analytical work on the US, Singapore, and Aotearoa health systems
- Real Indigenous health framework competence (Te Whare Tapa Whā, Te Pae Mahutonga), applied at the analytical level

There are six commercial buckets where that combination is genuinely competitive. The companies that fall into them are below, grouped and ranked.

## 6. Three honest cautions before you send anything

First, send one email at a time and watch what comes back. Do not send all ten at once. The wording is similar across them by design, and you will want to adjust based on what gets replies and what does not.

Second, the smokefree pitch to The Conversation and the KFF Health News pitch are the two with the most opening leverage, because they have a specific angle attached. Lead with those. The general "introduce myself for the freelance pool" emails are slower but more durable. Send the pitches first to learn what works, then send the introduction emails next week.

Third, on agency work (Real Chemistry, Klick): you will need one or two paid bylines from a smaller publication before they will look at you seriously. The order is: get a paid piece somewhere (The Conversation, BMJ Opinion, Vital Strategies blog), then apply to the agencies.

The rest is a matter of doing the outreach, paying attention to what gets replies, and following up after seven to ten days on anything that does not. Most of the upside is in the second message, not the first.
